# Voice & Tone

## In one sentence

Rooted, plain-spoken, proud-but-not-boastful — like the principal's letter in the brand book.

## What we sound like

- **Bilingual by default.** Every parent-facing surface speaks Punjabi and English. Punjabi script is first-class — not a translation pasted under English. On the mobile app the greeting is `ੴ ਸਤਿ ਸ੍ਰੀ ਅਕਾਲ`, not "Hello".
- **Concrete over abstract.** "PSEB-aligned curriculum strengthened by lab-based discovery" — not "world-class education for global citizens".
- **Quietly confident.** We have served Patiala since 2005. We don't say "premier", "elite" or "world-class". We say what we do.
- **Sangat-minded.** When a parent enrolls their child they join a community. We address them as part of the sangat — not as a customer.
- **Sikh-rooted but inclusive.** The school is Khalsa International. Sikh values are openly named. The school welcomes children of every faith — say so plainly when it matters; never compromise the identity to seem neutral.

## What we do not sound like

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| "World-class education" | "PSEB-aligned curriculum, strengthened by lab-based discovery." |
| "Empower future leaders" | "Children who walk out confident in their roots and ready to serve." |
| "Cutting-edge facilities" | List the specific facilities — labs, library, sports ground. |
| "We pride ourselves on…" | "We have served Patiala since 2005." |
| "Holistic development" | "Vidya — Vichar — Seva. Knowledge, clear thought, service." |

## Length & rhythm

- Web hero: ≤ 14 words.
- Section sub-heads: ≤ 8 words.
- Body paragraphs: 2–4 short sentences. Read it aloud — if it sounds stilted in either language, rewrite.
- Push-notification: ≤ 60 characters in each language.

## Honorifics

- The Principal is **S. Gurpreet Singh** in English; **ਸ. ਗੁਰਪ੍ਰੀਤ ਸਿੰਘ** in Punjabi. `S.` (for Sardar) is mandatory. Drop only when context already names him.
- Teachers: `Ms. Surjit Kaur` / `ਮੈਡਮ ਸੁਰਜੀਤ ਕੌਰ`. Honorifics persist in Punjabi.
- Parents: address as `ਸਤਿਕਾਰਯੋਗ ਮਾਪੇ` ("Respected parent") in formal notices; `Dear parent` is fine for transactional emails.

## Bilingual ordering rule

When both languages appear in the same block:
- Punjabi-first on Punjabi-primary surfaces (the mobile app greeting; the masthead's local-name line).
- English-first on bilingual print documents (matches PSEB convention).
- Visual hierarchy must reflect the choice — the primary language gets larger or bolder type.

## Examples

### Good
> ਅਸੀਂ ਆਪਣੇ ਸਕੂਲ ਨੂੰ ਇਮਾਰਤਾਂ ਜਾਂ ਬੈਨਰਾਂ ਨਾਲ ਨਹੀਂ ਮਾਪਦੇ।
> We do not measure our school by buildings or banners.

### Bad
> "Welcome to a world-class educational ecosystem!"
> (Generic. No Punjabi. Adjective stacking. Not us.)
